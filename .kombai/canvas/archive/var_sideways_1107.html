Sideways: the weight of a number, and where it sits

8 marks, 390px. The same four weeks with the figures in two places.

Weight, and whether the face has one

A bare numeral carries the value by itself, so it wants stroke weight. The catch is the stack: system monos often ship no bold face, so 700 is synthesised. At 11px that smears strokes and closes the counters of 6, 8 and 9. The top row is the numeral at the size it is used; the bottom row is the same face at 40px, where a fake bold is obvious.

Variant A — figures hug the bar

What the sheet does now. The figures sit inside the bar's own width, so they inherit its limits: no room for a word, and two numbers only fit when the bar is wide enough to separate them. Weight alone has to do the work of saying "this is a value".

Variant B — a rail of its own

The figures leave the bar and take a fixed 88px column, right-aligned, so they line up down the whole list and there is room for the word. "Free" and "paid" label the rows once each, and the Now / Prev headings cover both periods, which is what removes "just show the number". The bar keeps whatever width is left, and previous-period figures are washed because the row label already says which figure is which.

What the rail costs: 88px off every bar. At 390px the bar's usable length falls from about 250px to about 160px, so a difference of one cook is 6% of the bar instead of 10%. That is the trade — legible figures against a shorter scale.

What this changes

The figures stop being part of the mark and become a column of the list. That is a different object from a chart with labels: it is a table that happens to draw a bar, which is defensible for this data — the pairs are the point, and there are never more than 24 of them.
